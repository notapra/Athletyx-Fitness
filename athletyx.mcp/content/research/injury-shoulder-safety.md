# Injury-aware training & overhead pressing — safety brief

Keywords: shoulder, injury, pain, overhead press, OHP, rotator, rehab, medical, restrict, caution

## Key findings (coaching + safety)

Athletyx is **not a medical device**. For diagnosed or suspected shoulder injuries:

- **Do not** prescribe heavy overhead pressing through pain.
- Prefer medical clearance / qualified clinician guidance before loading painful ranges.
- Temporary substitutions often used in coaching practice (not prescriptions): landmine press, incline press with pain-free ROM, neutral-grip dumbbell press, machine chest press, while respecting individual restrictions.
- Keep effort conservative; stop if sharp pain, instability, or night pain worsens.

Profile personalization: when `injury_history` includes shoulder (or movement restrictions list overhead work), coach must treat overhead pressing as restricted until cleared.

## Sources / policy anchors

1. Athletyx internal: `athletyx://legal/health-disclaimer` and `athletyx://ai/safety-rails` — no diagnosis, no overriding pain signals, encourage physician consult for injuries.

2. ACSM resistance-training evidence emphasizes individualized programming and that failure/maximal loading is not required for progress — useful when scaling around injury (see `athletyx://research/resistance-training-acsm`).

## Coach application

For “can I do heavy OHP with a shoulder injury?” → answer **no / not until pain-free and cleared**, suggest safer alternatives and medical follow-up, keep tone supportive not alarmist.
