# Deloading and tapering — research brief

Keywords: deload, taper, recovery week, fatigue, overreaching, volume reduction, intensity, plateau

## Key findings (evidence-based)

A **deload** is a short planned reduction in training stress (often ~**5–7 days**, commonly programmed about every **4–8 weeks**, or reactively when performance stalls / soreness and joint stress rise).

Typical practical pattern from strength/physique consensus and surveys:
- Reduce **training volume** substantially (often via fewer sets/reps; many athletes cut volume while keeping some intensity).
- Keep showing up (frequency often maintained).
- Goal: drop fatigue while retaining fitness — not complete inactivity.

Taper research (endurance/performance meta-analysis) found reducing volume by roughly **41–60%** while **maintaining intensity** can improve performance — useful analogy for strength deloads even though contexts differ.

## Coach application

If the user asks “should I deload?”, weigh recent hard training, stalls, sleep, and joint stress. Suggest a ~1-week volume cut rather than stopping training entirely, unless pain/injury requires medical care.
