# Sleep, recovery, and training performance — research brief

Keywords: sleep, recover, recovery, fatigue, tired, readiness, rest, insomnia, deprivation

## Key findings (evidence-based)

- **Acute sleep loss** tends to impair exercise performance (meta-analytic estimates often around several percent worse across strength/power/endurance tasks), with effects depending on deprivation pattern and time of day.
- Sleep loss can raise **rating of perceived exertion** and reduce readiness even when motivation is high.
- Roughly **7–9 hours** of sleep is a common target for healthy adults; chronically sleeping ~**6 hours** is often suboptimal for hard training blocks.
- Practical advice: prioritize sleep hygiene, consider lighter technique/volume days after poor sleep, and schedule key lifts when better rested when possible.

## Coach application

If the user reports ~6 h sleep, say recovery is likely compromised for high-intensity work; suggest protecting sleep and reducing volume/intensity rather than pushing PRs.
